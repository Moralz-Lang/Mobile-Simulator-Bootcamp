# Final Mobile Incident Response Investigation

## Objective

Conduct a complete mobile incident response investigation using everything learned throughout the Mobile Security Bootcamp.

You will act as a Mobile Security Incident Responder investigating a potentially compromised Android device.

Unlike previous labs, this investigation contains both legitimate activity and suspicious indicators. Your goal is to determine whether the device is compromised, identify evidence, and recommend remediation.

---

# Scenario

A financial company's employee reports:

- Phone has become noticeably slower.
    
- Battery life has decreased dramatically over the past five days.
    
- Cellular data usage has doubled.
    
- Camera LED occasionally flashes unexpectedly.
    
- Several coworkers received strange SMS messages from the employee.
    
- The employee recently installed an APK outside of Google Play after clicking a company-related email.
    
- The phone is enrolled in the corporate MDM.
    

Your task is to determine:

- Was the device compromised?
    
- What evidence supports your conclusion?
    
- What actions should the security team take?

# Sample Executive Summary

> During the investigation, multiple indicators suggested that the Android device had been compromised by a malicious sideloaded application. The application requested excessive permissions, maintained persistent background services, generated unauthorized SMS activity, and communicated with external hosts. Battery statistics and network telemetry supported continuous background execution. Based on the collected evidence, the incident is assessed as **High Severity**, requiring immediate containment, credential rotation, forensic preservation, and reimaging of the device.

# Defensive Recommendations

Immediate

- Disconnect device from corporate network
    
- Preserve forensic evidence
    
- Notify security team
    
- Disable corporate accounts
    
- Rotate passwords
    
- Revoke tokens
    

Short-Term

- Remove malicious application
    
- Reimage device if necessary
    
- Verify MDM compliance
    
- Review audit logs
    
- Block malicious infrastructure
    

Long-Term

- Restrict APK sideloading
    
- Enforce application allowlists
    
- Enable Mobile Threat Defense (MTD)
    
- Strengthen phishing awareness training
    
- Require MFA for sensitive applications
    
- Continuously monitor mobile telemetry with MDM/EDR/IDR

# Knowledge Check

**Question 1**

What is the first priority during a mobile incident response investigation?

**Answer:**  
Preserve evidence while containing the threat, ensuring volatile data is not lost before analysis.

---

**Question 2**

Why are dangerous permissions not sufficient to prove malware?

**Answer:**  
Many legitimate applications require powerful permissions. Permissions become suspicious only when they are unnecessary for the app's intended function or are combined with abnormal behavior and other IoCs.

---

**Question 3**

Name three Android artifacts useful during an investigation.

**Answer:**

- Installed package list (`pm list packages`)
    
- Logcat logs
    
- Battery statistics (`dumpsys batterystats`)
    

Additional examples include running services, network statistics, permissions, and process lists.

---

**Question 4**

What framework helps defenders map attacker behavior to known techniques?

**Answer:**  
MITRE ATT&CK for Mobile.

---

**Question 5**

What are the four major phases of incident response?

**Answer:**

1. Preparation
    
2. Detection and Analysis
    
3. Containment, Eradication, and Recovery
    
4. Post-Incident Activity (Lessons Learned)
